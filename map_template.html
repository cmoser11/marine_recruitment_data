What the columns mean

Applicants
One row per person, not per record. The raw export repeats an applicant once for every lead source that touched them — up to six times — so those rows are collapsed first. In the alpha view this counts only applicants who passed, since alpha is measured among them.
Passing
AFQT of 31 or higher. Below 31 an applicant is mental group IV and needs an administrative review by the district commanding officer. The EPM's Table 3-6 minimum is actually tier-dependent — 21 for Tier I, 31 for Tier II, 50 for Tier III — but 31 is the working threshold used here.
Alpha
AFQT of 50 or higher — mental groups I through IIIA. Bravo is IIIB, 31 to 49. Per MCRCO 1100.1, IIIA starts at 50, not 51.
Raw rate
The plain share, straight from the data: applicants from that ZIP who cleared the bar, divided by applicants from that ZIP. Honest but jumpy — with two applicants it can only ever read 0%, 50%, or 100%, which is why it isn't what the map draws.
Adjusted
The raw rate pulled toward the command-wide average, by an amount that depends on how thin the ZIP's data is. A ZIP with 70 applicants barely moves; a ZIP with one applicant moves almost all the way to the average. The formula is (cleared + 8 × baseline) ÷ (applicants + 8), so a ZIP needs roughly eight applicants before it carries its own weight. This is what the map colors.
Odds vs. base
How that ZIP's adjusted odds compare with the command-wide odds. Odds are rate ÷ (1 − rate), so a 60% rate is odds of 1.5. Dividing the ZIP's odds by the baseline odds gives the multiplier: 1.00× is exactly average, 2.00× is double the odds. Double the odds is not double the rate — going from 50% to 67% doubles the odds.
ZIP income
Mean adjusted gross income per tax return in that ZIP, from IRS Statistics of Income 2022. It describes the economic conditions of the area an applicant comes from — it is not that applicant's or their family's income.

Rates are shrunk toward the baseline. Half of these ZIPs sent two applicants or fewer, where a raw rate of 0% or 100% means almost nothing. Each ZIP is pulled toward the command-wide rate in proportion to how little data it has, so a ZIP needs roughly eight applicants before it moves much on its own. The Adjusted column is what the map draws; Raw rate is the unadjusted share, shown so the difference stays visible.

The two maps say different things. Passing tracks education status — whether the applicant is a high school senior is the strongest single predictor. Reaching alpha tracks geography and income: ZIP mean income is the strongest univariate predictor of alpha, and applicants from the highest-income quartile of ZIPs reach alpha 63.8% of the time against 45.0% in the lowest.

Coverage. ZIP income is IRS SOI 2022 mean adjusted gross income per return. Positions are ZIP centroids from the Census gazetteer, so a bubble marks the center of a ZIP area, not a home address. State outlines are drawn from the straight survey borders and are for orientation only.
